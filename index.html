Consumer Trend Research

I did research on the topic of Caretaking in the Digital Age, which was published on February 2026.

It lists what digital technology are used to support unpaid caretaking, such as remote monitoring and medication management systems.

Around 27% of caregivers use medication management tools. 58% of caregivers over age 45 use at least one technology support. It helps us narrow down main users we’re targeting and the type of app that can be useful towards them.

Personas Research

Persona: Ethan Wesker

Ethan is a 56 year old caregiver who has to care for his wife Mia to treat her chronic illness. Ethan uses a digital medication systems tool to aid her health journey but limitedly, while Mia is reluctant since she lacks technological knowledge.

Ethan is Gen X. Many people in his generation understand how to use basic technological tools, but has a cautious approach and hesitates using technology that potentially invades their privacy. This could dissuade them from using products/service that needs their medical records or health data, especially ones that involves the usage of AI.

Behaviours:

  • 58% of caregivers over age 45 use at least one technology support, compared with 35% of those ages 18 to 44. This shows that older caregivers who provide more intensive care are more likely to rely on technology.
  • More than half (54%) of those providing six or more hours of care per week use at least one type of technology, compared to 46% of caregivers who provide care for fewer than six hours weekly.
  • Although hesitant, Ethan is reliant on technology in his daily routine with Mia.

Buying Priorities:

  • Common barriers that unpaid caretakers face include care recipients’ unwillingness or limited ability to use digital tools (23 percent); privacy or security concerns (20 percent); and lack of awareness about available options (18 percent).
  • Additional challenges, such as lack of time, insufficient technical support, and high costs, further hinder technology use.
  • Privacy and security are the two main concerns for Ethan. It also comes with awareness about available options, convenience, sufficient technical support, and low costs.

Actions:

  • 48% of unpaid caregivers use at least one type of technology to help provide care to their loved ones, primarily parents, minor children, friends or neighbours, and spouses or partners.
  • Medication management tools such as refill alerts and reminders are most common, with 27% of caregivers using them.
  • Ethan frequently uses technological tools such as medication reminders and refill alerts to remind Mia to take her medicine on time.

HMI Research

Two Problem Spaces

  1. Older adults and caregivers may have different levels of digital literacy, making some caregiving apps difficult to learn and use.
  2. Caregivers may be feel reluctant sharing health-related information through apps, especially when they don’t understand how their data is stored or used.

Two HMW Statements

  1. How might we make caregiving support feel effortless and seamlessly fit into everyday routines?
  2. How might we empower people receiving care to maintain control over their health data while using caregiving technologies?

Question 1–4

  1. Is it based on an existing problem or insight?

    • Yes. Caregiving is a huge responsibility, especially for unpaid caregivers who are often related to the patient. The job often involves multiple responsibilities and routines. With the advancement of technology, we can explore more ways to integrate technology with healthcare.

  2. Does it track a desired outcome?

    • Yes. The desired outcome is caregiving support that requires less effort, increases user satisfaction, and fits naturally into daily life.

  3. Is it written positively?

    • Yes. There is potential for greater integration as the world develops more advanced tools and newer generations become more tech-savvy.

  4. Is it broad enough to ensure many creative ideas?

    • Yes. It allows for many creative solutions, such as combining medication apps with physical devices, transportation support, health monitoring, and financial management.

Possible Project Directions

  1. Smart Medication Reminder. A physical system designed to help two user groups (caregivers and patients) coordinate medication routines. This benefits both groups by allowing caregivers to monitor medication routines anytime, anywhere, while giving patients greater autonomy and control over their own information.
  2. Smarts Care Communicator. A bedside communication device designed for people with limited mobility or difficulty using complex technology. This benefits both groups by reducing the need for frequent check-ins for caregivers, and allowing patients to communicate their needs more independently.

Project 1 Concept

Project 1 Concept

I will focus on designing the Smart Medication Box + Reminder from the perspective of the person taking the medication, also known as the Care Recipient.

The system consists of two connected components: an Arduino-based physical device and a mobile app. The physical device handles medication reminders and confirmation, while the app receives updates so the caregiver can monitor the patient's medication routine remotely. This allows each user to fulfil their responsibilities without requiring both to interact with the app equally.

When it is time to take medication, the Arduino will activate the OLED display, buzzer, and LED to show the current time and provide audible and visual reminders. The medication box will connect to the Arduino, using a light sensor to detect when the box is opened and an accelerometer to detect movement. After taking the medication, the Care Recipient will press a button on the device to confirm completion. The Arduino will then send this confirmation to the mobile app, which will update the medication status and notify the Accountability Partner with a message such as, “XX (Medication) was taken at XX AM.”

Although the concept makes use of several Arduino functions, I am still exploring which features are essential to the experience. In Project 2, I plan to investigate the technical feasibility of connecting the physical device to the app and test whether the interactions are simple and useful for both users.
